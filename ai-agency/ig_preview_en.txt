AI model prices keep falling — but the cheapest is not automatically the right one.

On September 21, xAI released Grok 4.7 at 2 dollars per million input tokens and 6 dollars per million output tokens — far below top-tier models such as GPT-5.6 Sol Max (4/20 dollars) and Fable 5.1 Max (10/50 dollars). xAI claims it is twice as fast.

What to note:
• On the independent Artificial Analysis index, Grok 4.7 scores 46, while Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 each score 53.
• On the agentic coding test Terminal-Bench 4.0 it reaches 26 percent, behind GPT-6 Astra (60 percent) and Fable 5.1 (55 percent).
• The takeaway is simple: a low price is no guarantee of top capability, and a high price is no guarantee of fit.

For your business, the question is not "which is cheapest" or "which is most famous", but:
1. Map the task first — routine and high volume, or complex and occasional.
2. Routine work can run on efficient models; complex work belongs with the most capable ones.
3. Measure the outcome, not only the cost. A bad match creates rework.
