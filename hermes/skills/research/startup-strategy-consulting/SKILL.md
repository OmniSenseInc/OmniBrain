# Startup Strategy Consulting (founder to launch)

Class-level workflow for coaching an early-stage founder from "losing
direction" to a public launch. Structuring the work as PHASE-GATED steps is
the core move: each phase produces a concrete deliverable and a gate the
founder must pass before advancing. Do not let the founder skip straight to
branding/publishing while the vision and business model are still fuzzy.

## The phase-gated roadmap

Never advance past a gate until its deliverable is locked. In practice for a
small AI/consulting startup:

1. **Ground truth** (facts, not vision): real revenue, runway, time budget,
   who holds the final decision (one name), honest hardest truth. No visioning
   until this is honest.
2. **Business inti / model**: what is the actual monetization (product vs
   service vs both) and HOW money enters. Confirm this from the founder's own
   words — see monetization pitfall below.
3. **Customer & problem**: beachhead (first niche) + single root cause, NOT a
   false one-of-N choice — see the clustered-pain pitfall.
4. **Vision / mission / values**: vision = long-term outcome WITHOUT product
   names or "small business" qualifiers unless the founder insists; mission =
   what is done now.
5. **Positioning**: one line for the target + differentiator + where the
   slogan sits. Keep it broad enough to span the brand's real reach.
6. **Validate** (before scaling): 10-15 real talks; gate is willingness to
   pay / commit. Often skipped by eager founders — the next phase partly
   substitutes for it.
7. **Pricing**: price by VALUE, not by close-to-your-cost. Prove the margin
   with a small calculator before accepting a number.
8. **MVP scope**: the smallest thing that makes the customer feel the value;
   ban "nice-to-have" features from scope.
9. **Internal SOP / sales flow**: who owns each funnel stage, pipeline
   stages, onboarding, client question bank.
10. **Soft launch / beta**: 3-5 pilot clients through the FULL funnel before
    broad public release.
11. **Public launch** (last): landing page, channels, content — only after
    gates above pass.
12. **Measure & iterate**: leads / consults / deals / churn, weekly.

Produce each phase as a single editable doc the founder takes away (not just
chat). File under a project dir; keep a status doc with checkboxes so both
sides see where they are.

## Coaching pitfalls (learned from corrections)

- **Do NOT force a founder to pick one pain when they have a real cluster.**
  If the target users genuinely feel 4 related pains together, that's a
  clustered pain, not indecision. Reframe it as ONE root cause with N faces
  (e.g. "messy manual data" => confusion over profit, stock, time, decisions)
  and let the solution attack the root. Forcing a single symptom to "narrow"
  contradicts market reality and erodes trust.
- **Confirm the actual monetization model before assuming.** A founder may
  say "free consult, then it's paid" and mean the BUILD/DEPLOY is paid while
  the consult is permanently free. Read back your understanding of the money
  flow and let them correct it before locking the business model.
- **Separate brand / product / consulting roles explicitly.** A founder often
  confuses "the company" (brand), "a product" (one vehicle), and "the
  consulting service." Get them to state which is the face, which is first
  revenue, which is the sticky product. This resolves most "losing
  direction" confusion.
- **Vision stays broad; beachhead stays narrow.** Vision should not say
  "small business" or a product name unless the founder insists — the
  company may span all businesses, with an F&B product as only the first
  wedge. Keep these two scopes separate and consistent.
- **Free assessment as lead magnet is table stakes.** Almost every AI
  consultancy offers free discovery/roadmap now. The differentiator is a
  quantified, repeatable readiness SCORE + gap analysis, not "free consult."
- **Price by value, and prove margin with numbers.** If a founder just says
  "250k covers cost," run a quick cost calculator (VPS + API + support hours
  + overhead). The hidden margin killer is SUPPORT TIME, not the headline
  cost — 1 support-hr/client/month often wipes most of the margin.

## Honest-limits stance

When a wall can't be legitimately crossed (login-gated data, blocked
scraping), say so plainly and offer the user-driven route (they browse on
their device and paste/screenshot) instead of engineering a ToS-violating
bypass. Never claim a push/deploy succeeded when the code/credentials aren't
in the current environment — report the blocker and give the exact setup
steps the founder/CTO runs on their own machine.
