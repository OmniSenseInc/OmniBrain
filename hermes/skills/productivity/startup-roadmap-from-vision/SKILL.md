# Startup Roadmap: from lost/vague to public launch

Use when a founder/adviser startup is "losing direction" or unclear on its vision and
wants a structured path to a public launch. Drive it as a phased, gate-gated process:
NO branding/publishing before the foundations are decided.

## Order matters — this is the whole trick
Most founders jump straight to product-building or public posting. The fix is to
sequentialize decisions behind gates. Work phases in order; do not skip.

0. GROUND TRUTH (honest current state — not visioning yet)
   - Money: current income, runway without revenue, real hours/week per person.
   - Who is the single decision-maker (must be one; CEO).
   - What the thing actually IS now (service? product? concept?).
   - The uncomfortable truth people avoid.
   Gate: can answer all honestly.

1. CORE BUSINESS DECISION
   - Is revenue product (recurring) or service (time-bound)? Pick roles: each line
     gets ONE role (revenue engine / lead magnet / long-term-value vehicle).
   - Resolve product-vs-jasa confusion explicitly.

2. CUSTOMER & PROBLEM — narrow it (the single hardest pushback)
   - Reject "everyone" and "all problems". If the founder insists all symptoms are
     real, find the single ROOT they share (e.g. "operates without clean connected
     data") rather than picking one symptom. Naming a root keeps scope broad while
     messaging stays focused.
   - Beachhead = the first niche to win, not the ceiling.

3. VISION / MISSION / VALUES (the payoff — but only after 0-2)
   - Vision = long-term outcome, NEVER a product name or a price. FNBSense-style
     products are vehicles, not the vision.
   - Mission = what you do daily. Keep values to 3-4.
   - If the founder has a slogan/tagline, DERIVE the vision/mission from it (keep
     consistent); slogan is the public short-form, vision is the internal compass.

4. POSITIONING (one sentence)
   - Template: "We are __ for __ such that __." Quote a real slogan as close/tagline.
   - Ground it against competitors; name the differentiators competitors lack.

5. VALIDATION (before building more) — talk to 10-15 real users, screen for budget
   + decision-maker, measure willingness to pay & willingness to install.
   Gate: >=5 say yes/willing + real price range. DO NOT let them skip this and
   launch; if skipped, force later beta to serve as validation.

6. PRICING — by VALUE, not by COST
   - Compute true unit cost INCLUDING support time & overhead (time is the real
     margin killer). Charge above it; target ~2-4x. If products are priced to
     barely cover VPS/API cost, flag it as a margin problem.

7. MVP SCOPE — smallest thing that makes the user feel value and pay
   - Make an explicit MUST vs DEFER list. "Nice-to-have" = enemy of release.

8. INTERNAL SOP — roles & sales pipeline
   - Stage pipeline (lead->qualified->consult->offer->won->onboard->retain), each
     with an owner. Chat with an LLM consultant should hand pricing/close to the
     human team, never close itself.

9. SOFT LAUNCH / beta (3-5 pilot businesses, full pipeline) then 10. PUBLISH then
   11. MEASURE.

## Pitfalls
- Free-consult / free-assessment is now the NORM in AI agencies — it is a ticket,
  not a differentiator. Differentiate with a measurable readiness score / product.
- Don't let the founder hand a product before the vision is decided (cart before
  horse). Products are vehicles.
- Reject "we serve everyone / every problem" answers by abstraction to a root,
  not by brute-force narrowing.
- When acting as an in-group consultant bot: never quote prices or close; the human
  team owns pricing/closing. Enforce it with an explicit railguard.

## Companion art
For AI agencies: paired free-diagnosis (readiness score) -> paid build; the free
assessment doubles as lead data. See the 'instagram-public-research' skill for the
